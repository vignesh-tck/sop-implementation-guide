Another way to think about a use case/tool, is to think about the kinds of recommendations we generate (based on the set of features I sent you/Bowie examples) and then think about the sets of barriers/incentives that would make it harder/easier to implement those recommendations. For example, if we recommend a park, we would want to understand any number of these things:

Are there any zoning laws/ordinances that might make it harder to create one
Are there any EPA policies that incentivize brownfield remediation (cleaning up polluted sites) that could be converted into a park
Are there local ordinances/policies tied to improving public health - that could help you lobby for a park (due to its positive impact on health)
Are there any local, regional, state, and/or federal grants that could be used to fund the park

But overall, I think the key is to define your "user story," considering the types of urban design changes we recommend, and then think through the three buckets, and then identify the types of factors that would impact the feasibility of implementing those recs, and then that helps you figure out what data sources you need to consider and what type of technologies/techniques are most appropriate to structure/make sense of that data.
